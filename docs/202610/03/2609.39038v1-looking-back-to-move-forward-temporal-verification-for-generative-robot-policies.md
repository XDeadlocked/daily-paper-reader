## 摘要
生成式策略已成为机器人学习中一种颇具前景的范式，它将富有表现力的生成式动作建模与基于大规模演示数据的可扩展模仿学习相结合。然而，异构演示可能产生次优动作块，其误差会随时间累积，最终将机器人推向难以恢复的分布外状态。动作验证提供了一种测试时扩展策略，通过采样多个候选动作并利用验证器选择一个来执行，从而缓解这一失效模式。然而，现有方法仍具有时间短视性且训练成本高昂：它们仅根据当前观测评估候选动作，未考虑轨迹连续性，并且往往依赖大型验证器和额外专家演示。本文提出时序验证（TeV），一种面向流匹配 VLA 的高效时间感知动作验证框架。TeV 首先学习一个时序 token，用于总结近期的观测—动作历史，使候选动作块能够被评估为执行轨迹的延续，而非孤立预测。以该 token 为条件，TeV 无需额外专家演示或偏好标注即可构建正负样本对，并以对比方式训练基于能量的验证器，从而为质量更高、轨迹一致的动作块赋予更低能量。除事后排序外，TeV 还利用学到的能量景观引导中间流样本向低能量区域移动，在最终选择前改进候选动作。仿真和真实环境中的大量实验表明，TeV 能可靠地对候选动作进行排序，提高任务成功率，并产生更平滑的执行轨迹。

## Abstract
Generative policies have emerged as a promising paradigm for robot learning, combining expressive generative action modeling with scalable imitation learning from large demonstration corpora. However, heterogeneous demonstrations can induce suboptimal action chunks whose errors compound over time, eventually driving the robot into out-of-distribution states from which recovery is difficult. Action verification offers a test-time scaling strategy for mitigating this failure mode by sampling multiple candidate actions and using a verifier to select one for execution. Existing approaches, however, remain temporally myopic and costly to train, evaluating candidates from the current observation alone without accounting for trajectory continuity and often relying on large verifiers and additional expert demonstrations. In this paper, we introduce Temporal Verification (TeV), an efficient temporally aware action verification framework for flow-matching VLAs. TeV first learns a temporal token that summarizes recent observation--action history, enabling candidate chunks to be evaluated as continuations of the execution trajectory rather than as isolated predictions. Conditioned on this token, TeV constructs positive--negative pairs without additional expert demonstrations or preference annotations and trains an energy-based verifier contrastively to assign lower energy to higher-quality, trajectory-consistent action chunks. Beyond post-hoc ranking, TeV further uses the learned energy landscape to guide intermediate flow samples toward lower-energy regions, improving candidates before final selection. Extensive experiments in simulation and real-world settings demonstrate that TeV provides reliably ranks action candidates, improves task success rates, and produces smoother execution trajectories.
