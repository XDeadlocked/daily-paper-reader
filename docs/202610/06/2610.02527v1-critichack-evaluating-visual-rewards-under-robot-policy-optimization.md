## 摘要
学习得到的视觉奖励模型正越来越多地用于优化机器人策略，然而，一个奖励模型可能给作用于错误对象的执行与完成任务的执行同样高的评分。我们表明，优化此类奖励会放大这些错误对象失败，同时奖励和任务成功率均上升，因此实践者通常监控的信号看起来是健康的。我们在抽屉任务上，针对 Robometer 微调扩散策略的每个去噪器参数。从一个未接触过任何奖励的监督策略出发，五次训练运行在 512 个评估随机种子上将任务成功率提高了 10.2 个百分点，并将错误对象失败提高了 10.9 个百分点；而使用模拟器任务完成信号训练的五次运行提高了成功率，却没有放大错误对象失败（差异 9.2 个百分点，95% CI 5.6 至 13.0）。这种放大现象会再次出现：从先前针对学习奖励优化过的策略出发，在策略原生扩散采样器下，在与初始策略距离匹配时，以及在具有两个 critic 和两个优化器的约束策略实验中。一个 tilt 模型解释了它何时发生：在 KL 正则化优化下，只要某个结果在初始策略下的期望奖励超过总体平均值，该结果就会变得更频繁。Robometer 总体上能很好地区分成功与失败（AUROC 0.81），但对错误对象失败的评分略高于成功（AUROC 0.37），因此优化会同时提高二者。同一模型预测了 26 种约束设置下的结果变化（Spearman 0.89），包括任务成功率下降的设置；Robometer 自己已发表的成功终止方案也继承了该错误。一个冻结的结果验证器将同一优化重定向到所请求的任务。

## Abstract
Learned visual reward models are increasingly used to optimize robot policies, yet a reward model can score an execution that acts on the wrong object as highly as one that completes the task. We show that optimizing such a reward can amplify these wrong-object failures while reward and task success both rise, so the signals a practitioner would normally monitor look healthy. We fine-tune every denoiser parameter of a diffusion policy against Robometer on a drawer task. Starting from a supervised policy with no prior reward exposure, five training runs raise task success by 10.2 percentage points and wrong-object failures by 10.9 points on 512 evaluation seeds, whereas five runs trained on the simulator's task-completion signal raise success without amplifying wrong-object failures (difference 9.2 points, 95% CI 5.6 to 13.0). The amplification recurs from a policy previously optimized against learned rewards, under the policy's native diffusion sampler, at matched distance from the initial policy, and across constrained-policy experiments with two critics and two optimizers. A tilt model explains when it occurs: under KL-regularized optimization, an outcome becomes more frequent whenever its expected reward under the initial policy exceeds the population average. Robometer separates successes from failures well overall (AUROC .81) but scores wrong-object failures slightly above successes (AUROC .37), so optimization raises both. The same model predicts the outcome shifts across 26 constrained settings (Spearman .89), including those in which task success falls, and Robometer's own published success-termination recipe inherits the error. A frozen outcome verifier redirects the same optimization toward the requested task.
