ABSTRACT
Flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies generate action chunks by
transporting samples from a task-agnostic isotropic Gaussian source.
As this
source is conditioned on neither recent execution nor predicted future evolution,
(i) it discards the local continuity established by recently executed motion. (ii)
Even when predictive world representations are introduced, they often only con-
dition the transport dynamics rather than determine where generation starts, how
far it may deviate, or along which action directions it may expand. Building on
this observation, we introduce PROACT, a world-calibrated proposal-to-action
framework that makes the generative source itself predictable. (i) To preserve
motion continuity, a lightweight Proposal Expert converts recent actions into a
scene-aware hypothesis via one motion-anchored endpoint flow-matching step,
initializing generation near the demonstrated action manifold. (ii) To jointly cap-
ture intended scene evolution and proposal–future compatibility, a prospective
World Expert treats the hypothesis as a soft motion prior while predicting the
task-consistent latent future. (iii) From this compatibility, the model calibrates
a proposal-centered anisotropic source, where a bounded per-step extent con-
trols the allowed deviation and a trace-normalized low-rank geometry under a
condition-number budget allocates refinement over coupled translation, rotation,
and gripper directions. Compared with π0.5, PROACT improves performance
across simulation and real-world tasks while reducing denoising steps by 50%,
inference latency by up to 25.8%, and increasing throughput by up to 34.8%.

INTRODUCTION
Vision-language-action (VLA) models Black et al. (2024); Bjorck et al. (2025); Li et al. (2026b);
Luo et al. (2026); Shao et al. (2025) increasingly use diffusion Chi et al. (2025) or flow matching Lip-
man et al. (2022) to generate continuous robot actions, building on broader progress in fine-grained
multimodal understanding and visual grounding Peng et al. (2026); Shao et al. (2024; 2023; 2019).
However, these generative decoders typically initialize each action chunk from a generic source dis-
tribution Intelligence et al. (2025); Zheng et al. (2026), forcing the policy to repeatedly transport
uninformed samples toward executable trajectories, as illustrated in the upper panel of Fig. 1. Such
a generic source ignores readily available context that could inform where action generation should
begin. By making the source distribution itself contextually predictable, action generation can begin
from a more informed region of the action space, yielding a policy that is both more accurate and
substantially cheaper to sample during closed-loop policy execution.
A generic isotropic source leaves two useful sources of information unexploited. The first is re-
cently executed motion. Although task-level behavior can be highly multimodal, physical motion
often exhibits strong local regularity over short horizons, and recently executed, reliable actions are
already available during closed-loop control Black et al. (2026); Wang et al. (2026a). A naive strat-

(2026). Yet such continuation becomes unreliable around contact, reorientation, gripper actuation,
or task-phase transitions, where appropriate motion may deviate sharply from recent behavior Yang

treated as a proposal to be evaluated against the intended future, rather than as the action itself.
The second is predicted future evolution, which provides prospective evidence for calibrating such
a proposal. Prospective world modeling Zhao et al. (2025); Zhang et al. (2025); Tian et al. (2025);
Zhang et al. (2026); Li et al. (2026f); Zhu et al. (2026a); Li et al. (2026c) has increasingly been
incorporated into VLA policies, but typically as auxiliary supervision or additional conditioning.
In these formulations, prospective information helps predict what should happen next, but is not
explicitly used to calibrate an existing action hypothesis according to whether it remains appropriate
for the anticipated task evolution. The missing element is therefore an explicit interface between a
candidate motion, prospective world reasoning, and generative refinement.
We build this missing interface around a propose–calibrate–refine principle and instantiate it as
PROACT, a World-Calibrated Proposal-to-Action Flow framework.
PROACT first constructs a
scene-aware motion hypothesis, then uses prospective world reasoning to estimate the correction
required to realize the intended future, and finally converts this calibration into the source distribu-
tion for flow-based action generation, as illustrated in the lower panel of Fig. 1.
Specifically, (i) a lightweight Proposal Expert first converts recently executed actions into a scene-
aware motion hypothesis. It extrapolates short-horizon motion and uses it to initialize the motion-
anchored flow module, which grounds the proposal in the current VLM context through a single
endpoint flow step. Rather than producing an executable action, this stage places generation near a
plausible region of the demonstrated action manifold and provides a soft motion prior for subsequent
reasoning. (ii) A prospective World Expert calibrates this proposal against the task-consistent fu-
ture. The motion hypothesis is provided as contextual evidence for prospective reasoning, while the
prediction target remains the demonstrated future encoded in the latent space of a frozen V-JEPA 2
encoder Assran et al. (2025). Calibration readouts are further supervised by the residual between
the demonstrated action and the proposal, forcing the shared world representation to encode both
the task-consistent latent future and the proposal–future compatibility. (iii) The resulting calibration
is finally expressed as a proposal-centered anisotropic source for action generation. A bounded
per-step extent limits how far generation may deviate from the proposal, while trace-normalized
low-rank geometry determines along which coupled action directions that correction should occur.
An Action Expert then transports samples from this calibrated source into executable action chunks
using the base VLA’s original flow parameterization. Thus, PROACT changes where and how action
generation begins while preserving the flexibility to depart from the proposal when needed.

• We formulate generative action prediction from a propose–calibrate–refine perspective, where re-
cent motion provides a soft local hypothesis calibrated by task-consistent prospective reasoning,
providing an interface between local motion continuity and future-aware action generation.
• We introduce an asymmetric proposal-aware World Expert that uses motion to assist future pre-
diction while anchoring the desired future independently of proposal correctness, and jointly
predicts proposal-specific refinement extent and geometry at every horizon step.
• We replace the isotropic Gaussian of flow-based VLAs with a world-calibrated, proposal-
centered source while leaving their transport parameterization unchanged.
• We validate PROACT across extensive simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic manipula-
tion tasks, where it consistently outperforms strong baselines while reducing denoising steps by
up to 50%, demonstrating both superior effectiveness and inference efficiency.

along which coupled directions it should proceed at each horizon step.

EXPERIMENTS
We evaluate PROACT through four questions: (Q1) Does the world-calibrated, proposal-centered
source outperform strong VLA baselines? (Q2) Are gains larger when long-horizon or perturbed
conditions weaken motion continuity? (Q3) Which propose–calibrate–refine components drive the
gains? (Q4) Does calibration improve proposal refinement and generation efficiency?
3.1
COMPARISON ON SIMULATION BENCHMARKS

brated source improves strong VLA baselines under saturation, distribution shift, and long-horizon
bimanual control across increasingly demanding manipulation conditions.

ABLATION STUDY
This section addresses Q3 through component ablations in simulation and the real world.
Motion-informed source. Tab. 4 separates motion continuity from scene grounding. Reusing the
last chunk is slightly worse than Gaussian noise, whereas motion extrapolation gains 0.6/3.3 points
on LIBERO/real-world tasks. Proposal Expert grounding adds another 0.6/3.4 points, showing that
recent motion is a useful soft prior but needs scene grounding when local continuity is unreliable.
World calibration. Tab. 5 validates the asymmetric calibration interface. Removing the World
Expert causes the largest drop, by 1.9 points on LIBERO and 8.0 points in the real world. Re-
moving proposal conditioning, future supervision, or source calibration costs 1.3/5.4, 0.9/4.0, and
0.6/2.7 points, respectively. Thus, proposal-aware future reasoning and source calibration are both
necessary, especially when real-world motion continuity becomes unreliable. The larger real-world
penalties highlight their importance under physical manipulation settings.
Refinement extent and geometry. Tab. 6 shows that the two calibration outputs are complementary.
Relative to a fixed isotropic proposal-centered source, adaptive extent improves LIBERO/real-world
success by 0.9/3.3 points, while low-rank geometry contributes 0.4/2.0 points. Combining both
raises the gains to 1.5/6.0 points. Extent controls correction magnitude, while geometry allocates it
across coupled action dimensions. Their complementary gains show that effective refinement needs
both adaptive magnitude and structured directionality, especially across embodiments, real-world
contact transitions, and long-horizon manipulation settings in practice.

RELATED WORK
Beyond embodied control, multimodal learning has also explored fine-grained understanding and
media manipulation grounding Peng et al. (2026); Shao et al. (2024; 2023). These lines of work
provide complementary perspectives on grounding visual semantics across modalities.
Structured Priors for Action Generation. Recent generative robot policies increasingly inject
structured priors to simplify action synthesis Hu et al. (2026); Li et al. (2026g;e;d). These priors
improve task efficiency. Diffusion and flow-based policies typically generate actions from a generic
Gaussian source Chi et al. (2025); Black et al. (2024); Intelligence et al. (2025); Bjorck et al. (2025);
Zheng et al. (2026); Luo et al. (2026). Recent work instead explores more informative starting
points. Streaming Flow Policy Jiang et al. (2025) initializes generation around recently executed
actions, while VITA Gao et al. (2026) directly transports visual latents toward action representations.
A2A Jia et al. (2026) further encodes historical proprioceptive sequences as an informative flow
source. Together, these studies establish source design as an important dimension of generative
action policies. However, an informative source does not ensure that its implied motion remains
suitable as the task evolves. PROACT therefore treats recent motion as a proposal and determines
how strongly and along which directions it should be corrected.
World Models for Action Generation. A complementary line of work augments action policies
with predictive world representations to account for future scene evolution Zhu et al. (2026a); Li
9


et al. (2026c); Zhu et al. (2026b). DreamZero Ye et al. (2026), LingBot-VA Li et al. (2026b),
DreamVLA Zhang et al. (2026), and WorldVLA Cen et al. (2025) couple future prediction or world
modeling with action learning, while X-WAM Guo et al. (2026) incorporates future spatial structure
through RGB-D prediction. VLA-JEPA Sun et al. (2026) introduces latent future prediction into
VLA learning without explicit pixel-space generation, with related predictive objectives explored
in UP-VLA Zhang et al. (2025). Fast-WAM Yuan et al. (2026) further suggests that explicit test-
time future generation is not always necessary. Together, these studies leave open how predictive
information should most effectively influence action generation. In PROACT, the predicted future
calibrates the proposal-centered source, controlling how far and along which directions the proposal
is refined across diverse tasks, horizons, and embodiments.
5
CONCLUSION
We presented PROACT, a world-calibrated proposal-to-action framework that makes the source of
flow-based VLAs context dependent. A scene-aware motion proposal and task-consistent future cal-
ibrate refinement extent and geometry while preserving the original flow parameterization. Across
simulation and real-world benchmarks, this design improves robustness under perturbations and
long-horizon execution while reducing denoising and inference cost, motivating prospective source
calibration for robust and efficient generative robot policies across manipulation settings.

A.2
TRAINING DETAILS
A.2.1
EPISODE BOUNDARY HANDLING
Two boundary cases are handled by masking rather than by discarding samples. Near the end of
an episode, the future clip is completed by repeating the last available frame. Because temporal
attention would allow these padded frames to contaminate every latent token, such samples are
excluded from Lfuture while keeping all remaining losses.
Near the beginning of an episode, the action history is shorter than M. The missing entries and the
motion state are initialized to zero, while gripper dimensions are initialized to the gripper value of
the first executed action. At deployment, the motion state is recomputed from scratch over the last
M executed actions at every call, ensuring that the analytical proposal is identical during training
and inference for consistency across repeated evaluations.

C.2
TASK DESCRIPTIONS
We deploy PROACT on GALAXEA R1 Lite and AgileX Cobot Magic and evaluate the same six
sequential manipulation tasks on both platforms. The suite spans repeated object sorting, container
interaction, articulated-object manipulation, retrieval, and contact-rich wiping, jointly testing visual
grounding, precise grasping and placement, phase transitions, and robustness to accumulated execu-
tion error. Each task is evaluated through the ordered progressive checkpoints below, with identical
semantic completion criteria across the two embodiments throughout evaluation.
• Block Sorting. Instruction: “Place the red and blue blocks into the target bowl.” This task
evaluates repeated object-centric pick-and-place over a multi-stage sequence, requiring the
policy to preserve color identity, maintain the prescribed manipulation order, and recover a
reliable grasp after completing the first placement. The four progressive stages are: 1) Pick
Red Block—securely grasping and lifting the red block; 2) +Place Red in Bowl—placing
the red block inside the target bowl; 3) +Pick Blue Block—locating and grasping the blue
block after the scene has changed; and 4) +Place Blue in Bowl—placing the blue block
inside the target bowl to complete the task.
• Pot Lid Retrieval. Instruction: “Remove the lid and retrieve the object from the pot
onto the plate.” This task tests interaction with a covered container and the transition
from opening to retrieval. It requires the policy to remove the lid without destabilizing
the pot, visually re-ground the previously occluded target, and transfer it to a constrained
destination. The two progressive stages are: 1) Remove Lid—grasping and moving the lid
until the pot is accessible; and 2) +Retrieve Object to Plate—extracting the target object
and placing it stably within the designated area of the plate.
• Drawer Manipulation. Instruction: “Open the drawer, place the toy inside, and close the
drawer.” This task combines articulated-object control with object transport and evaluates
whether a policy can maintain task coherence across distinct interaction modes. The four
progressive stages are: 1) Open Drawer—grasping the handle and opening the drawer
sufficiently; 2) +Grasp Toy—releasing the handle and securely grasping the toy; 3) +Place
Toy Inside—placing the toy fully inside the open drawer; and 4) +Close Drawer—closing
the drawer to its terminal state without displacing the inserted toy.
• Drawer Toy Retrieval. Instruction: “Open the drawer, retrieve the toy, place it on the
plate, and close the drawer.” This complementary drawer task tests target discovery un-
der partial occlusion, collision-aware extraction from a confined space, and restoration of
the articulated object after retrieval. The four progressive stages are: 1) Open Drawer—
opening the drawer until the interior is accessible; 2) +Retrieve Toy—grasping and extract-
ing the toy from the drawer; 3) +Place Toy on Plate—transferring the toy onto the plate;
and 4) +Close Drawer—returning the drawer to its closed state.

LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
While PROACT improves generative action prediction by calibrating motion-informed proposals
with prospective world representations, several limitations remain.
Counterfactual supervision. The current formulation is trained primarily from factual expert tra-
jectories, where each action sequence is paired with the future evolution that actually occurred.
As a result, the model does not explicitly observe the counterfactual world evolution that would
be induced by alternative or erroneous proposals. The calibration module therefore learns how a
proposal should be adjusted under expert future supervision, rather than directly comparing the pre-
dicted consequence of a proposal against the desired task-consistent future. A promising direction
is to incorporate proposal perturbations, simulator rollouts, or action-conditioned world models to
provide explicit counterfactual supervision and enable consequence-aware calibration.
Fixed-horizon prospective reasoning. Although PROACT is supervised with a temporal future
sequence rather than a single endpoint, its prospective reasoning is currently performed over a fixed
horizon aligned with the action chunk. However, different manipulation stages may require different
temporal scales: fine-grained contact and alignment can often be resolved from short-term evolution,
whereas the consequences of decisions in long-horizon tasks may only become evident after several
action chunks. Extending the current formulation toward multi-scale or adaptive-horizon world
reasoning could allow the policy to calibrate proposals using both immediate interaction cues and
longer-term task progress across diverse manipulation phases and tasks.
