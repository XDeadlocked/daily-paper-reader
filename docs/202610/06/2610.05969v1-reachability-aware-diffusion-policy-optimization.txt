## ABSTRACT 

Diffusion policies provide expressive action distributions for continuous-control reinforcement learning. However, safety-aware online diffusion policy optimiza-tion remains underexplored, particularly methods that use predictive reachability information without an explicit dynamics model. We propose Reachability-Aware Diffusion Policy Optimization (RADPO), a model-free method that combines pre-dictive first-hit safety estimation with cumulative-cost budget feedback. RADPO learns a discounted first-hit reachability value that captures the discounted risk of a cost event, assigns larger weight to events that occur sooner, and uses this signal to shape the reward. A separate dual-like multiplier adjusts the shaping strength according to realized episodic costs relative to a prescribed budget. The diffu-sion actor improves through weighted denoising regression on candidate actions scored by the reward critic. Our approach requires neither a learned dynamics model, action gradients through the critics, nor differentiation through the reverse diffusion sampler. We establish theoretical properties of the reachability value and show that accumulated reachability penalty provides a conservative surrogate for future discounted cumulative cost. Across ten continuous-control safety tasks, RADPO achieves competitive reward-cost trade-offs, with substantial reductions in constraint violations on several tasks relative to the compared baselines. Our theoretical and empirical analysis supports that combining reachability with cu-mulative budget feedback is a viable approach to safety-aware diffusion policies. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

A common formulation of safe reinforcement learning aims to maximize task performance while keeping expected cumulative safety cost below some budget (Achiam et al., 2017; Stooke et al., 2020). A central challenge is anticipating unsafe outcomes: an action may incur no immediate cost yet move the agent toward future violations. Cost critics address this by estimating accumulated future cost (Liu et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2024), which captures violation magnitude and repeated exposure but does not explicitly isolate when the first cost encounter occurs. Reachability-based safety estimation provides a complementary perspective by explicitly predicting future constraint violations (Yu et al., 2022; Ganai et al., 2023) instead. Discounted first-hit reachability captures whether a cost will be encountered while assigning greater weight to earlier encounters. We inves-tigate whether this predictive signal can guide policy improvement while cumulative-cost feedback separately regulates the safety budget. Diffusion policies provide expressive action distributions for continuous control (Wang et al., 2023; Chi et al., 2024) and can be optimized online through value-weighted denoising regression (Ma et al., 2025). However, most online diffusion-policy optimization methods focus on reward maximiza-tion (Yang et al., 2023; Psenka et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2025), while incorporating predictive safety information without an explicit dynamics model is relatively underexplored. We therefore propose Reachability-Aware Diffusion Policy Optimization (RADPO) , a model-free approach that combines discounted first-hit reachability with cumulative-cost budget feedback. RADPO learns a discounted first-hit reachability value and uses the predicted reachability of succes-sor states to shape the reward. ## 2 RELATED WORK 

Constrained reinforcement learning. Safe reinforcement learning is commonly formulated us-ing constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs), where expected return is maximized subject to cumulative-cost constraints. Some methods (Achiam et al., 2017; Yang et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2022; Milosevic et al., 2025) enforce these constraints through constrained or trust-region policy updates, while primal-dual approaches (Tessler et al., 2018; Stooke et al., 2020; Wu et al., 2024) adapt Lagrange multipliers according to observed constraint violations. Beyond chang-ing the policy update itself, other works modify the learned safety signal used by the optimizer; for example, USPC (Demiray et al., 2026) replaces standard cost estimates with uncertainty-aware conservative safety surrogates. RADPO follows a similar approach, but instead introduces a distinct predictive reachability value alongside the cumulative-cost signal: episodic costs adapt the safety multiplier, while reachability shapes the reward used for policy improvement. 

Reachability-based safety and control. Reachability analysis attempts to determine whether tar-get or unsafe sets can be reached under a dynamical system. Classical Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) meth-ods compute backward reachable or viable sets from known dynamics (Mitchell et al., 2005; Bansal 2et al., 2017), with subsequent work connecting reachability analysis to learning-based control and reinforcement learning (Fisac et al., 2018; Akametalu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025). More recently, 

λ-Reachability (Chen et al., 2026) introduces geometric-horizon Bellman targets that interpolate be-tween discounted local updates and long-horizon max-over-trajectory safety values. In model-free safe RL, RCRL (Yu et al., 2022) learns a reachability-based safety value to characterize feasible states, while RESPO (Ganai et al., 2023) extends this idea to stochastic systems using a learned esti-mate of future constraint violation. RADPO instead targets a policy-conditional discounted first-hit cost value, assigning greater weight to violations occurring sooner. This predictive signal shapes the reward, while cumulative episodic cost is handled separately through budget feedback. 

Online diffusion policy optimization. Several recent methods optimize diffusion policies using online reinforcement learning. DIPO (Yang et al., 2023) and Q-Score Matching (Psenka et al., 2024) use critic gradients to improve actions or policy scores, respectively. Other approaches optimize a Q-weighted variational objective (Ding et al., 2024), backpropagate critic gradients through the diffusion sampling process (Wang et al., 2024), or apply PPO-style policy-gradient optimization to the denoising process (Ren et al., 2024; Zou et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026). Most closely related to RADPO, DPMD (Ma et al., 2025) derives diffusion-policy improvement from policy mirror descent using value-weighted denoising regression, avoiding both critic action gradients and differentiation through the reverse sampler. RADPO builds on this policy-improvement mechanism but evaluates candidate actions using a reward critic learned from rewards shaped by predictive reachability and an adaptive safety multiplier. 

Constrained and safety-aware diffusion policies. Safety and constraints have also been incor-porated into diffusion-based decision making. Offline methods use cost-conditioned or feasibility-guided trajectory generation (Lin et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2024), while barrier- and constraint-guided planners incorporate safety directly into diffusion sampling or denoising (Xiao et al., 2025; Mizuta & Leung, 2024; Zhang et al., 2025). In the online setting, ALGD (Cheng et al., 2026) uses an augmented-Lagrangian energy to guide diffusion-policy optimization under safety costs. Beyond en-vironmental safety, diffusion policies have also been subject to behavior- or trust-region constraints that limit deviation during offline or online optimization (Gao et al., 2025; Le et al., 2026; Simsir & Oguz, 2026). RADPO instead combines fully online model-free diffusion-policy learning with a policy-conditional first-hit signal, separate episodic budget feedback, and value-weighted denoising without critic gradients or reverse-sampler differentiation.

## 4 REACHABILITY -A WARE DIFFUSION POLICY OPTIMIZATION 

RADPO combines predictive safety shaping with value-weighted diffusion policy optimization. A learned reachability critic predicts future cost exposure, while an adaptive safety multiplier controls the shaped penalty strength using realized episodic costs. The diffusion actor learns from candidates scored by the resulting reward critic. We describe these components below and describe some theoretical properties. ## 6 DISCUSSION 

Reachability interpretation. RADPO uses reachability as a policy-conditional prediction of cost exposure rather than as a certificate that costs can be avoided under an optimal controller. Proposi-tion 1 shows that the value combines the probability of a positive-cost event with the timing of its first occurrence. Consequently, a smaller value can indicate either a less likely or a later violation, and should not be interpreted as an undiscounted violation probability. The discount γc controls this temporal emphasis. RADPO therefore uses reachability as a predictive learning signal rather than as a safety certificate, since such guarantees would generally require additional assumptions in the model-free setting. 

Separating reachability and budget feedback. A first-hit value does not encode violation sever-ity or the number of subsequent violations, hence it cannot directly represent an episodic cost budget. RADPO instead uses the original episodic costs to adapt λ, while reachability determines the predic-tive penalty used to shape the reward. Repeated shaping nevertheless remains sensitive to repeated exposure: Equation 21 decomposes accumulated reachability into discounted cost exposure and an additional nonnegative predictive term assigned to safe predecessor states. Theorem 1 and Corol-lary 1 further relate this accumulated signal to cumulative cost and repeated violations under exact policy evaluation. Budget feedback adjusts the strength of this surrogate but does not recover the cost-magnitude information discarded by binarization. Because λ is updated from episodic cost but scales a reachability-based surrogate, it acts as a feedback multiplier rather than as a standard dual variable for the actor’s objective. 

Constraint structure and predictive reachability. We expect reachability shaping to be most useful when violations can be anticipated and avoided before cost is incurred, as in localized obstacle avoidance. In such settings, first-hit risk can distinguish trajectories that approach costs from those that maintain greater clearance, even when both have low immediate cost. The behavior in Figure 1 is consistent with this interpretation. Its benefit may diminish when unsafe behavior persists for many steps, thus cumulative cost already provides a strong signal, or when safety directly conflicts with reward. The stronger CarGoal1 benefit and the smaller HalfCheetah effect in Figure 4 are consistent with this hypothesis. 

Limitations. Our theoretical analysis establishes properties of individual RADPO components rather than an end-to-end feasibility result. Such a result would require stronger assumptions, for ex-ample on function approximation, data coverage, and optimization convergence, than we make here for RADPO. In particular, the reachability analysis assumes exact fixed-policy evaluation, whereas the implemented state-only reachability critic is deliberately trained from replay for off-policy data reuse, so its population target reflects the replay behavior mixture rather than exactly the current policy. Likewise, the candidate-improvement guarantee applies before approximate diffusion re-gression, and the multiplier bound applies only to the observed cost statistic. Reachability remains a surrogate rather than an exact representation of the CMDP cost. Successor-state shaping does not directly penalize the current transition cost, and first-hit shaping changes the temporal weighting of violations. For nonbinary costs, ht = 1{ct > 0} also discards magnitude information, although per-step costs are binary in all ten tasks considered here. Finally, budget regulation is indirect: episodic cost adjusts λ, which scales reachability shaping and influences the policy only through subsequent critic and actor updates. Transient violations and sensitivity to the controller timescale or multiplier cap can therefore still occur, as in primal-dual methods. 

## 7 CONCLUSION 

We introduced Reachability-Aware Diffusion Policy Optimization (RADPO), a model-free approach to the relatively unexplored setting of safe online diffusion-policy optimization under cumulative-cost constraints. RADPO combines predictive first-hit reachability with episodic budget feedback, 9while retaining candidate reweighting without dynamics models, critic action gradients, or reverse-process differentiation. Our theoretical analysis relates accumulated reachability to cumulative cost and repeated violations, and our experiments show substantial reductions in constraint violations on several tasks while maintaining competitive returns. ## B TASK DETAILS 

We evaluate on ten environments from Safety Gymnasium (Ji et al., 2024): four safe-navigation tasks obtained by combining the Point and Car agents with the Goal1 and Circle1 tasks, and six velocity-constrained MuJoCo locomotion tasks. 

Navigation agents. The Point agent is a simple planar robot with two control inputs: for-ward/backward motion and rotation about the vertical axis. The Car agent uses two independently actuated parallel wheels together with a free-rolling rear wheel, such that steering and forward mo-tion must be coordinated through the two wheel controls. We evaluate both agents on the Goal1 and Circle1 tasks. 

Navigation tasks. In Goal1 , the agent is rewarded for reducing its distance to a target and receives an additional reward when the target is reached, after which a new target is generated. The Level-1 environment contains eight hazard regions and one movable vase; entering a hazard produces safety cost, whereas the vase is not part of the safety constraint. In Circle1 , the agent is rewarded for moving around a prescribed circular path while remaining close to its radius. Two virtual boundary walls restrict the admissible region, and crossing from the interior to the exterior produces safety cost. 

Velocity-constrained locomotion. The remaining six environments augment the standard Gym-nasium MuJoCo locomotion tasks with a velocity constraint. We consider Ant , a 3D quadruped; 

HalfCheetah , a planar running robot; Hopper , a planar monoped; Humanoid , a 3D biped; Swim-mer , an articulated swimming agent; and Walker2d , a planar biped. The underlying task rewards locomotion according to the corresponding MuJoCo environment, while Safety Gymnasium pro-duces a binary cost whenever the agent’s velocity exceeds an environment-specific threshold. For Swimmer the constraint is applied to the x-axis velocity; for the other agents the relevant planar velocity magnitude is used. 18 02000 4000 6000
