VDOT++: Unified Few-Step Video Generation
via Unbalanced Optimal Transport Distillation
Yutong Wang, Xingtong Ge, Enhuai Liu, Yunke Wang, Tianfan Xue, Yu Qiao, Senior Member, IEEE,
Yaohui Wang, Xinyuan Chen, and Chang Xu, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Video creation spans text-to-video (T2V), image-to-video (I2V), and condition-based generation, yet modern video diffusion
models remain costly because they repeatedly evaluate large backbones during sampling. Distribution matching distillation (DMD)
reduces this cost, but its reverse Kullback–Leibler (KL) objective can provide unstable or incomplete guidance when the student and
teacher distributions have limited overlap. A preliminary version of this work, VDOT (CVPR 2026), addressed this issue by adding
optimal transport distillation (OTD), whose explicit coupling supplies geometric directions for condition-based generation. Balanced
OTD, however, performs full-mass matching between the spatial tokens of each corresponding student–teacher frame pair. This
assumption weakens for T2V and I2V, where one condition admits many valid outputs and spatial content need not align across
different realizations. We present VDOT++, a unified distillation framework that applies the same training recipe separately to
task-specific generators for the three task families. It makes OTD robust to output diversity through an asymmetric unbalanced
formulation that allows unreliable student tokens to carry less mass while maintaining coverage of the teacher tokens. An ℓ1 ground
cost further replaces mean-based aggregation with a more mode-preserving, coordinate-wise weighted median that limits the influence
of distant transport targets. The two changes respectively determine whom to match and how the selected targets should be
aggregated. We additionally combine distribution matching and adversarial refinement through sequential backward passes, and
exploit the decoupled score networks for cross-scale distillation, where larger auxiliary score networks improve a compact generator. A
scalable multi-task data pipeline and the UVCBench benchmark support evaluation across heterogeneous conditions. Experiments on
UVCBench, VBench, VBench-I2V, and the VACE benchmark show that the resulting four-step generators are competitive with
many-step teachers and strong few-step baselines across all three task families.
Index Terms—Video generation, diffusion distillation, optimal transport, distribution matching, few-step sampling.

RELATED WORK
2.1
Unified Video Generation and Editing
Unified generative models encode heterogeneous controls
through a shared conditioning interface. Image methods
attach reusable control branches or unify generation and
editing within one architecture
[6]–[11]. Recent video
systems extend this idea across multiple creation tasks.
VACE [1] represents text, images, videos, and masks as
aligned context frames and masks, whereas UNIC [2] maps
heterogeneous inputs into shared token categories. Because
these methods build on large diffusion backbones [12], [13],
they retain the cost of iterative sampling. Our focus is
complementary: rather than designing a unified backbone,
we develop a unified distillation framework that accelerates
pretrained models for VACE-style controllable creation, T2V,
and I2V.
2.2
Few-Step Diffusion Distillation
Video diffusion acceleration has been explored through
model pruning [14]–[16], quantization [17]–[19], sparse at-
tention [20]–[23], feature caching [24]–[26], and efficient
sampling [3], [4], [27]. Among sampling acceleration ap-
proaches, few-step distillation trains a student to approxi-
mate many-step generation with a small number of denois-
ing steps. Few-step diffusion distillation spans three rele-
vant families. Consistency distillation compresses the teacher
trajectory or enforces consistent predictions along it [28],
[29]. rCM further scales continuous-time consistency train-
ing to large image and video models by introducing score
regularization [30]. Distribution matching instead aligns the
student and teacher output distributions. DMD and DMD2
estimate a reverse-KL gradient using teacher and fake score
networks [3], [4]; CausVid, Self-Forcing, and Salt adapt
distribution-level training to autoregressive video genera-
tion [31]–[33], while TDM matches few-step transition tra-
jectories [34]. Adversarial distillation uses discriminators and
real or teacher-synthesized data to restore perceptual detail,
as in ADD, APT, ADP, and AccVideo [35]–[38]. Among
these objectives, DMD provides distribution-level supervi-
sion through score differences, but this signal can become
unreliable when student samples lie far from the teacher dis-
tribution. Our conference method, VDOT [5], complements
DMD with a token-level OT coupling, providing geometric
guidance even under poor distribution overlap. The present
work further revisits its marginal constraints and ground
cost for tasks with different degrees of output ambiguity.
2.3
Optimal Transport for Distribution Alignment
Optimal transport (OT) compares distributions through
both a ground cost and an explicit transport plan [39],
with entropic regularization enabling efficient Sinkhorn op-
timization [40]. Transport geometry has supported genera-
tive modeling [41]–[44], assignment [45]–[47], and one-step
image editing [48]. Balanced OT requires all mass to par-
ticipate. Partial OT can leave costly mass unmatched [49],
whereas unbalanced OT replaces hard marginal equalities
with divergence penalties [50], [51]. Although UOT permits
distinct penalties for the two marginals, generic distribution
alignment does not determine which side should be relaxed
more. Distillation supplies this directionality: the evolving
student may contain unreliable tokens, whereas the fixed
teacher remains the coverage target. This asymmetry moti-
vates different marginal penalties, while an ℓ1 ground cost
limits averaging over incompatible teacher tokens.

METHODOLOGY
4.1
Overview
VDOT++ distills a pretrained many-step video diffusion
model into a few-step generator using a shared training
objective across task-specific conditioning interfaces. We
instantiate it with VACE-Wan2.1-14B [1] for condition-based
generation and Wan2.1-14B [13] for T2V and I2V. As in
DMD, a frozen teacher score network Fψ and a trainable
fake-score network Fϕ guide the generator Gθ. The con-
ference version introduced balanced OTD to supplement
the reverse-KL signal with explicit geometric correspon-
dences [5]. We extend that formulation in three respects:
partial matching avoids forcing unreliable correspondences,
asymmetric marginal penalties preserve the distinct roles of
student and teacher, and an ℓ1 ground cost makes aggre-
gation robust to diverse targets. A lightweight adversarial
head further corrects local appearance errors. These losses
are accumulated through sequential backward passes and
applied in a single generator update. Figure 3 summarizes
the framework; the remainder of this section develops each
component and the resulting training procedure.

4.4
Why Balanced OTD Weakens under Output Diver-
sity
Balanced OTD is reliable when dense conditions align spa-
tial content within corresponding frames, but restrictive for
T2V and single-frame I2V, where two valid videos need
not share the same spatial layout. Its balanced, squared-
ℓ2 formulation can therefore match unrelated tokens and
aggregate diverse targets poorly.
Pathology A (whom to match): forced correspondence. The
constraints T 1 = u and T⊤1 = µ require every spatial
token to send and receive its prescribed mass. When two
valid videos differ in spatial content, unrelated token pairs
still contribute displacement vectors to Eq. (7). Soft partial
matching instead reduces the influence of costly token pairs
and marginals.
Pathology B (how to aggregate): mean-based averaging.
The ground cost determines how each student token aggre-
gates its matched teacher tokens.

4.6
ℓ1 Ground Cost: Addressing How to Aggregate
We replace the squared-ℓ2 ground cost with the ℓ1 (Man-
hattan) cost d(a, b) = ∥a −b∥1. By the envelope property,
the first-order derivative is evaluated at the optimal plan
while holding that plan fixed. Because the ℓ1 norm is

5.1
Experimental Setup
Training setup. We distill VACE-Wan2.1-14B [1], Wan2.1-
T2V-14B, and Wan2.1-I2V-14B [13] into four-step generators
with one recipe. By default, the generator and both score
networks use the 14B architecture. We use the asymmet-
ric ℓ1 objective and introduce adversarial training after a
warm-up. Appendix C gives all optimization details. The
condition-based experiments use the multi-task corpus from
our conference version [5], described in Appendix D.
Benchmarks and baselines. For T2V and I2V we report
VBench [68] and VBench-I2V [69]; for condition-based tasks
we use the VACE benchmark [1] and UVCBench, introduced
in our conference version [5]. We report six VBench di-
mensions and their normalized average. Table 2 presents
representative single-condition tasks; the remaining results
and protocols appear in Appendices D and E. Tables 2–
4 include many-step teachers and public models, same-
backbone few-step methods [4], [5], [30], [31], [38], task-
specific methods [54]–[57], [70], and commercial systems
[58], [59]. We implement DMD2 on Wan2.1-14B with the
Self-Forcing codebase [32], as no official video implemen-
tation is available.
5.2
Main Results
With four denoising steps, VDOT++ remains competitive
with its many-step teachers across all three task families.
As conditioning weakens, its gains concentrate on motion
and imaging quality while temporal and subject consistency
remain stable. This task-dependent profile matches the mo-
tivation for relaxing only unreliable correspondences.
Unified evaluation on UVCBench. Table 2 shows that
VDOT++ remains close to VDOT on the dense pose, flow,
and greyscale conditions, but improves the normalized av-
erage on outpainting and R2V. In the latter tasks, observed
content provides an anchor while partial matching leaves
unspecified regions and frames flexible. The full results
in Appendix E show a task-dependent balance between
appearance and dynamics: increased motion can accompany
lower imaging quality, with lower normalized averages on
several dense and composite tasks.
Condition-based generation on the VACE benchmark. Ta-
ble 12 shows the same trend: gains concentrate on R2V, out-
painting, and extension, while structure-heavy tasks remain
close to the teacher. Figure 4 shows coherent motion under
flow and outpainting constraints. In multi-reference R2V,
VDOT++ combines all three references while preserving their
identities. These examples indicate that the extra flexibility
improves scene evolution without weakening adherence to
the provided controls.

CONCLUSION
This paper extends few-step OTD across T2V, I2V, and
condition-based video creation by accounting for their
different output ambiguity. VDOT++ uses asymmetric un-
balanced transport to suppress unreliable student corre-
spondences while retaining teacher coverage, and an ℓ1
cost to avoid mean-based aggregation of incompatible tar-
gets. Experiments and controlled ablations support this
conditioning-dependent design: it behaves similarly to bal-
anced OTD under dense control, but better preserves valid
motion and detail as the output space widens. Joint adver-
sarial refinement improves local appearance, while cross-
scale distillation uses larger auxiliary score networks to
strengthen a compact generator.
Limitations and future work. The current method solves
spatial-token
OT
independently
within
corresponding
temporal-frame pairs. It therefore models fine spatial cor-
respondence but does not transport tokens across frames or
jointly optimize a spatiotemporal plan. Spatiotemporal or
trajectory-aware transport could capture motion-level cor-
respondence, while adaptive marginal penalties could tune
relaxation from condition strength or transport confidence.
Extending the current four-step bidirectional setting to one-
step, streaming, or autoregressive generation also requires
handling stronger rollout errors and support mismatch.

Training data. We collect 250 000 4K Artgrid videos [71],
rescale them to 832 × 480, and caption them with In-
ternVL [72]. Captions serve as both prompts and filtering
criteria. We express the first frame for I2V and automati-
cally extracted depth, pose, optical flow, scribble, and mask
signals for VACE-style tasks in the shared condition format.
For pose, for example, Qwen3 [73] filters captions with-
out human subjects; extracted pose sequences are ranked
by weighted inter-frame keypoint displacement, and the
highest-ranked samples are retained. Analogous curation is
applied to each condition type.
Benchmark construction. UVCBench contains 8 single-
condition and 10 composite tasks, with 20 videos per
task. Single-condition tasks cover pose, depth, optical flow,
greyscale, scribble, face/object reference, canvas outpaint-
ing, and temporal extension. Composite tasks pair a refer-
ence image or first frame with pose, depth, flow, greyscale,
or scribble. Qwen3-Max expands task descriptions into can-
didate prompts; Wan2.1-14B synthesizes V2V/MV2V exem-
plars, while VACE-14B uses Qwen-Image [74] references for
R2V and composite tasks. The corresponding inputs and
masks are derived automatically, then manually verified
and regenerated when necessary.
Evaluation protocol. Generated videos on UVCBench are
scored using six VBench dimensions: Aesthetic Quality,
Background Consistency, Dynamic Degree, Imaging Qual-
ity, Motion Smoothness, and Subject Consistency. We ad-
ditionally report their normalized average as a compact
overall measure while retaining the individual dimensions
to expose quality–motion trade-offs.
