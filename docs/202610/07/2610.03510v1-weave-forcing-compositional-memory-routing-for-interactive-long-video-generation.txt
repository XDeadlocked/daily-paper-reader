## ABSTRACT 

Recent advances in autoregressive video generation have improved temporal con-sistency over extended durations, yet interactive storytelling requires more than continuous scene extension: a new shot may combine characters and backgrounds from different historical shots. Whole prompt retrieval can overlook the distinct reference needs of individual components, while directly combining all histori-cal memories may introduce unrelated visual content. To address these problems, we present Weave Forcing , a training-free framework for compositional memory reuse in interactive long video generation. First, we use an LLM for semantic slot routing to decompose user prompts into character and background descrip-tions and explicitly select suitable historical references for each component. To isolate the required content, masked memory weaving uses contrasting attention maps conditioned on semantic slots to construct refined semantic masks, selec-tively exposing relevant tokens from compressed historical KV memories to guide the generation of the current shot. We further introduce coverage adaptive RoPE to adjust temporal offsets and memory retention according to no, partial, or full ref-erence coverage, addressing visual artifacts observed when incomplete historical references are positioned close to the current generation. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Weave Forcing improves cross-shot subject and background con-sistency while maintaining competitive visual quality and text alignment. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Video diffusion models have rapidly advanced visual realism, motion complexity, and text align-ment (Kong et al., 2024; Wan et al., 2025). Autoregressive models further enable efficient, tempo-rally consistent long video generation through sequential prediction, historical context reuse, and improved training and denoising (Deng et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2026). However, filmmaking and interactive storytelling require users to revise instructions, switch shots, and recall earlier characters or backgrounds. Interactive multi-shot generation must therefore respond to new instructions while preserving continuity with relevant historical content (Lin et al., 2023). Existing approaches can be distinguished by the temporal attention structure of their video gen-erators. Methods built on bidirectional diffusion transformers (DiTs) produce high-quality clips and support composition across shots through joint generation or conditioning on historical frames. However, repeatedly denoising each target clip with bidirectional attention incurs substantial com-putational cost and high generation latency, limiting rapid user feedback (Guo et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2026a). Causal autoregressive approaches instead generate sequentially and reuse historical KV states; combined with few-step sampling, they reduce latency while preserving long-range con-text and motion dynamics (Yin et al., 2025; Meng et al., 2026). Within this causal paradigm, global historical context and local within-shot context, together with shot-transition control, further support cross-shot consistency and interactive video generation (Lu et al., 2026; Luo et al., 2026b). These advances improve interactive generation, but precise reuse of historical content for each new shot remains unresolved.

Weave Forcing selectively reuses relevant historical content (just right). Two limitations arise when instructions combine content from different narrative moments, as shown in Figure 1. First, historical references can provide too much unrelated information or too little relevant evidence. Reusing compressed or sampled representations of preceding frames preserves whole-scene content, including unrequested contents (Zhang et al., 2025). Conversely, selecting a single source by global prompt similarity, as in the optional automatic router of Echo Forcing, can miss required components across shots (Wu et al., 2026a). For example, an instruction may bring a previously seen girl and boy into a library, with each character and the background originating from a different shot. Reusing all three source memories also imports unrelated content, whereas the most similar single shot may omit a required reference. Additional sources alone cannot resolve within-shot selection. Second, a uniform reference use policy struggles to reconcile two competing demands: preserving content supported by historical references and generating new content with-out such support. Strong historical conditioning can constrain unreferenced components, whereas indiscriminately weakening it can compromise the consistency of referenced characters and back-grounds. Infinity-RoPE demonstrates that manipulating temporal rotary position embedding (RoPE) coordinates can regulate temporal continuity and enable scene transitions (Yesiltepe et al., 2026). In-spired by this positional control, we adapt temporal RoPE settings to the availability of character and background references, balancing reference preservation with flexible generation of new content. To address these challenges, we propose Weave Forcing , a training-free framework for composi-tional memory reuse in interactive long video generation. Semantic slot routing uses a large lan-guage model (LLM) to first perform slot prompt splitting to identify the requested characters and background, and then apply cross-shot routing to associate each component with a historical source and its corresponding target slot prompt. Masked memory weaving uses contrastive slot attention (CSA) to distinguish target content from other content within the same historical memory, producing a semantic KV mask that selectively exposes relevant tokens. The references are combined in the cross-shot cache to guide the new shot. Coverage adaptive RoPE then adapts temporal positioning and reference retention to the available character and background support, while progressive mem-ory decay smooths the transition toward the current shot’s own context. Together, these components enable selective recombination of historical content as user instructions evolve. Experiments on 100 six-shot prompts demonstrate the effectiveness of Weave Forcing, showing improved cross-shot subject and background consistency over the Self Forcing backbone while achieving competitive visual quality and text alignment. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce Semantic Slot Routing , which decomposes each shot prompt into character and background slots and independently matches them to available historical source slots, enabling composition of references from different shots. • We propose Masked Memory Weaving , which constructs semantic KV masks through CSA, then combines the independently masked historical KV memories to retain requested content while suppressing unrelated characters and backgrounds. • We develop Coverage Adaptive RoPE , which adapts temporal offsets and reference reten-tion to no, partial, or full character and background reference coverage, balancing historical guidance with the generation of newly requested content. 22 RELATED WORK 

Early work on autoregressive video generation connected causal sequence modeling with diffusion through independent per-token noise levels, enabling generation beyond the training horizon (Chen et al., 2024). Subsequent approaches reduced sampling cost through distillation or adversarial post-training (Yin et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2026), while training on self-generated histories and rolling-window denoising helped reduce error accumulation (Huang et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026). Comple-mentary system-level optimizations improve the efficiency of streaming deployment and long video training (Feng et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026). Interactive generation additionally requires responsiveness to changing instructions while maintain-ing temporal coherence. Chunk-wise prompting controls successive segments (Teng et al., 2025), while LongLive (Yang et al., 2025) refreshes cached states under new prompts to improve transi-tions. Other work adapts positional encoding and cache management for extended rollouts and scene transitions (Yesiltepe et al., 2026), or constrains teacher supervision to balance responsiveness with stability (Wu et al., 2026c). Extending interaction to multi-shot narratives further requires memory across shot boundaries: global and local contexts support streaming generation across shots (Luo et al., 2026b), and Echo Forcing (Wu et al., 2026a) uses hierarchical memory for scene recall. In contrast, our work addresses compositional recall by routing character and background slots to his-torical sources, masking unrelated KV content, and adapting memory positioning.

## 4 METHOD 

4.1 SEMANTIC SLOT ROUTING 

Cross-shot reference reuse requires identifying the historical content needed by the current shot. Retrieval based on whole prompt similarity can miss characters and backgrounds originating from different shots. Selecting the top-k shots alone does not resolve this issue, since global similarity neither assigns sources to individual components nor specifies which content to retain. We therefore use an LLM to establish these correspondences: slot prompt splitting extracts character and back-ground descriptions, and cross-shot routing matches each component to a historical source and its target slot prompt as shown in Figure 2 (left). This assignment supports composition across shots and identifies the relevant content within each source.

Bottom: full coverage. 4.3 COVERAGE ADAPTIVE ROPE As the current shot accumulates context, historical guidance must be regulated. Following the tran-sient reference reuse of Echo Forcing (Wu et al., 2026a), we remove loaded cross-shot references after a prescribed number of completed blocks, rather than upon reaching the window capacity: one block for no coverage and five for partial or full coverage in our configuration. This eviction frees space for within-shot history without removing the sink window itself. The sink, compressed, and recent windows maintain rolling anchors, compressed history, and recent context, respectively, while the current window holds the block being denoised. Their updates support the transition from historical guidance to the current shot’s own context. The similarity-adaptive decay rule is detailed in Appendix B.5. However, a uniform retention policy is insufficient because referenced and unreferenced content re-quire different positional treatment. We therefore distinguish no coverage, partial coverage, and full coverage according to character and background reference availability, with corresponding temporal RoPE configurations and retention policies (Figure 3). 

No coverage. When both character and background reference sets are empty, we use the preceding shot’s complete compressed memory as a transient style reference without semantic masking. We assign a temporal RoPE offset of 45 to separate it from the current queries and remove it after the first block. Subsequent generation relies on the Intra-Shot Cache, preserving stylistic continuity while limiting unrelated historical influence. 

Partial coverage. When only one semantic category has references, we insert the corresponding masked memories into the Cross-Shot Cache. Since uneven reference support across semantic com-ponents may cause drift, we use a temporal RoPE offset of 45 to increase its positional separation from the current queries. The reference then decays progressively until eviction, preserving guidance for the supported component while allowing missing content to develop from the current prompt. 

Full coverage. When both semantic categories have references, we use their masked memories with similarity-dependent temporal RoPE offsets. We set the offset within [1 , 6] based on the similarity between the reference and current-shot prompt embeddings. This close positional coupling facili-tates the joint reuse of character and background references, while progressive decay before eviction supports the transition to the current shot’s own context. 65 EXPERIMENTS 

Dataset. Using Gemini 2.5 Pro, we construct 100 test narratives by generating concise prompts and expanding each into six detailed shot prompts. Each shot lasts 10 seconds, yielding 60-second videos. Appendix A.1 provides the expansion template. 

Implementation Details. We build Weave Forcing upon Self Forcing (Huang et al., 2026) with the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B backbone (Wan et al., 2025) and use Gemini 2.5 Pro (Google, 2025) for slot prompt decomposition and causal cross-shot reference routing. We generate videos at a resolution of 832 × 480 pixels with random seed 0 and configured denoising timesteps [1000 , 750 , 500 , 250] .The model generates three latent frames per autoregressive block, with a total KV cache capacity of 21 latent frames, allocated to the sink, compressed, recent, and current windows with capacities of 12, 3, 3, and 3 latent frames, respectively. All experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU with 96 GB of memory. 

Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate subject and background consistency at both intra-shot and inter-shot levels. We sample frames separately for intra-shot and inter-shot evaluation. We then use YOLO (Redmon et al., 2016) for subject localization and SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023) for segmenta-tion, separating subject regions. DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) features are then extracted from the corresponding regions and compared using cosine similarity to measure visual consistency. We use VBench-Long (Huang et al., 2024; 2025) to assess aesthetic and imaging quality on each complete video. For text alignment, we apply the VBench ViCLIP metric (Wang et al., 2024) to each shot and its corresponding short raw prompt (Appendix C). 5.1 QUANTITATIVE RESULTS 

Table 1 compares Weave Forcing with representative bidirectional and autoregressive baselines. Bidirectional models exhibit stronger intra-shot consistency, likely benefiting from access to the full shot context when modeling dependencies among frames during generation. Although Weave Forcing does not lead in intra-shot consistency, it achieves the highest inter-shot subject and back-ground consistency among the compared methods, demonstrating its strength in preserving both characters and backgrounds across shot boundaries. This supports the effectiveness of selectively reusing cache components relevant to the current instruction, rather than relying on a single histor-ical source or uniformly sampled historical frames. Alongside these cross-shot consistency gains, it maintains competitive video quality, including imaging quality and text alignment. We attribute 7this in part to our coverage adaptive RoPE strategy, which adjusts the use and progressive decay of historical references according to the available character and background support, helping mitigate visual drift and unstable transitions. Overall, the quantitative results demonstrate the effectiveness of selective cross-shot memory reuse in preserving historical content while maintaining competitive visual quality and alignment with the current prompt. Figure 4 shows a representative interactive generation example. The first three shots establish indi-vidual characters, while later prompts require switching scenes and combining different characters. Self Forcing does not explicitly support free shot-level switching; in this example, its generated frames are dark, making their visual content difficult to discern. Methods such as LongLive can generate content that clearly deviates from the prompt, as marked by the red circles. We further observe that methods such as EchoShot and Rolling Forcing may match the requested scene but still alter referenced details such as clothing materials or trouser colors, as highlighted by the yellow boxes. In contrast, Weave Forcing better preserves these appearance details when composing characters drawn from different historical shots. This behavior is consistent with seman-8tic slot routing and masked memory reuse, which provide relevant visual references while limiting interference from unrelated content. ## 6 CONCLUSION 

We presented Weave Forcing, a training-free framework for compositional memory reuse in inter-active long video generation. Semantic slot routing uses an LLM to select historical sources for individual characters and backgrounds, while CSA constructs semantic KV masks to combine rel-evant memory content with reduced interference from unrelated components. Coverage adaptive RoPE adjusts temporal offsets and reference retention to the available support, balancing historical continuity with new content generation as memory decay shifts guidance toward the current shot’s own context. Experiments demonstrate improved inter-shot subject consistency while maintaining competitive visual quality and text alignment. These findings suggest that effective memory reuse for interactive video generation should prioritize the relevance of individual semantic components to the current instruction, rather than treating historical scenes as indivisible visual references. 9AI USE STATEMENT

17 A.4 LLM I NFERENCE COST 

Each LLM request uses approximately 4,000 tokens in total, with an average reported throughput of about 130 tokens per second (TPS). Dividing this token budget by the throughput gives a coarse latency estimate of 4,000 /130 ≈ 31 seconds, or roughly 30 seconds per call. This estimate is indicative rather than a measured end-to-end latency: TPS characterizes token generation, whereas the total token budget also includes input tokens. Actual request latency therefore depends on the input–output token split and API serving overhead.

B.4 SEMANTIC SEPARATION 

Semantic separation determines whether character and background content drawn from the same historical frame are treated as one shared memory source or as independent semantic components. 19 Without separation, a source frame selected for both roles is inserted once and its K/V tokens remain jointly available. Consequently, subject appearance and scene layout are recalled together, which is appropriate when the complete historical composition should be preserved but may also transfer irrelevant background or subject attributes. With separation enabled, the shared frame is represented by role-specific views of the same stored K/V tensors. Let Ms,r be the semantic mask for role r in source shot s. The routed memory for that role is eKs,r = Ms,r ⊙ Ks, eVs,r = Ms,r ⊙ Vs, (20) or, equivalently in attention implementation, excluded tokens receive a strong negative attention bias (Cheng et al., 2022). Character and background may therefore share the same source frame while contributing through different spatial token subsets. The distinction is solely whether co-located semantic content is kept coupled or separated; it does not change the selected historical frame or create new visual features. ## E LIMITATIONS AND FAILURE ANALYSIS 

Figure 9 illustrates a failure mode in a crowded multi-subject shot. Although the generated scene retains both people, their appearance attributes are not cleanly separated: the lower part of the rear male subject is rendered with skirt-like clothing, while the female subject inherits the male subject’s hat. This is an attribute-entanglement (Tang et al., 2023) error rather than a complete failure to render either subject. The failure has two coupled causes. First, an imperfect semantic mask can retain spatial tokens from an adjacent subject, leaving insufficient separation between their respective historical appearances. Second, the new prompt asks the generator to attend to many entities and visual attributes simultane-ously. The resulting competition for attention can weaken the slot-specific routing signal and cause the model to bind an attribute to the wrong person (Chefer et al., 2023). These examples suggest that more discriminative instance-level masks and explicit multi-subject binding are needed when several visually salient subjects must be recalled and modified in the same shot. 24
